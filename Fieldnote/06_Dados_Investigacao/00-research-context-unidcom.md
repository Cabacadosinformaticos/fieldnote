# Research context: UNIDCOM/IADE and who Fieldnote is for

## Question

Who would use a platform like Fieldnote in our own institution, what that research unit does, and
what its needs change in the requirements. The course briefing describes proposal 11 in general
terms. This file explains why the team framed it around a concrete user, UNIDCOM/IADE, and what
that framing does and does not allow us to claim.

## Why we framed the project around a real research unit

The briefing asks for "uma solução baseada numa arquitetura distribuída que resolva uma necessidade
real" (a solution based on a distributed architecture that solves a real need). Proposal 11
describes the need in the abstract: researchers who create studies, define activities and follow
participants, and participants who record text, photo, video or audio. It names possible uses
(consumer experiences, use of products or services, everyday behaviour, experiences of places) but
no user.

A platform designed for "researchers in general" has no one to check its requirements against.
We wanted one
group whose work we could read about, whose constraints we could name, and against whom each
requirement could be checked. The obvious candidate is in the same building: UNIDCOM/IADE, the
design and communication research unit hosted at IADE, Universidade Europeia. Through informal
contact at IADE, the team learned that UNIDCOM would use an application to run mobile ethnography
and diary studies, and that a platform of this kind could become part of one of its research
projects. We have no written statement and no named project, so we treat this as context, not as a
requirement.

Two points need stating first:

1. The briefing does not mention UNIDCOM. The link is the team's framing of proposal 11, not a
   requirement of the course. Neither "UNIDCOM" nor "IADE" appears in the briefing text.
2. No public UNIDCOM page found in September 2026 mentions Fieldnote, a diary-study tool or a
   request for one. Everything below about how UNIDCOM could use the platform is our inference
   from what the unit publishes, and it is written as such.

## What UNIDCOM is

### Identity and funding

| Fact | Source and evidence |
|---|---|
| Name | Unidade de Investigação em Design e Comunicação (UNIDCOM/IADE). The Universidade Europeia site calls it "the research unit for Design and Communication at Universidade Europeia" |
| Host | "It is managed by the non-profit association EUROPEIA ID and is based at IADE – Universidade Europeia" (UNIDCOM, Vision and mission) |
| Founded | "Established in 2000, UNIDCOM was accredited by the FCT in 2003 in the field of Arts and Humanities" (same page) |
| Current funding | FCT project UID/00711/2025, January 2025 to December 2029 (FCT SciPROJ record). The previous cycle was UIDB/00711/2020 (2020 to 2024) |
| Last published rating | "In the 2019 FCT evaluation, UNIDCOM received a "Very Good" rating, scoring 12 out of 15 points in the Arts and Humanities panel" (Vision and mission). The rating of the 2023/2024 evaluation was not found on a public page |
| Size | 131 researchers, of whom 58 are integrated members and 73 collaborators, as stated on the unit's site when accessed |

The FCT (Fundação para a Ciência e a Tecnologia) is the Portuguese public agency that evaluates
and funds research units for several years at a time. A tool is useful to the unit only if it fits
the kind of research the unit reports.

### How its research is organised

UNIDCOM states that "Instead of creating separate research groups, we have embraced a broad
research program revolving around 5 thematic clusters". The five clusters are Crafting Cooperation
and Coexistence (C3), Rethinking for Regeneration and Resilience (R3), Thoughtfulness Technology
for Thrive (T3), Exploring Economic Ethos (E3) and Finding Flourishing Futures (F3). The same page
still describes an older structure of seven clusters, which included "Interaction Design and UX",
"Design for social innovation and sustainability" and "Communication, Culture and Consumption", and
the list of ongoing projects is still organised by those seven. We read the site as being in
transition and do not depend on either list.

Its laboratories are listed as SDI.Lab (Strategic Design and Innovation), BiD.Lab (Bio-Inspired
Design), DTN.Lab (Design-Tech Nexus), DFH.Lab (Design for Humanity) and MADx.Lab (Media Arts and
Design). Two descriptions are relevant to us:

- DTN.Lab "operates at the intersection of engineering, computer science, technology, art, and
  design". It is the lab closest to a software tool built by computer engineering students.
- DFH.Lab "operates at the intersection of social sciences, including sociology, anthropology,
  public policy, and design". Anthropology is where ethnography comes from.

The labs page also still describes a User Experience Lab: "The User Experience Lab (UX.Lab) is a
laboratory dedicated to the study of perceptive and cognitive aspects of interaction, user
experience (UX), usability, interaction design". Its listed equipment includes a usability
laboratory, eye tracking and "Tablets with IOS and Android operating system". We could not confirm
whether UX.Lab still exists under that name.

### Its research outlets

- The Senses & Sensibility international conference, held since 2003. The 12th edition,
  "Senses & Sensibility'23: Design And Complexity", took place in Málaga, Spain, in 2023.
- The Design Doctoral Conference, held yearly.
- The journal ThRAD (The Radical Designist), which the unit describes as "currently not active".
- Edições IADE (IADE Press), for books.

## Where diary studies and mobile ethnography fit in UNIDCOM's work

We read the unit's lists of ongoing projects and publications looking for work that collects data
from people in their own context, over time. We did not find a project that uses a diary-study or
mobile-ethnography tool. We did find work whose methods are close to it:

| Work | What it does | Why a diary tool could serve it |
|---|---|---|
| SMARTOUT, Smart Public Park Solution: Development of Real-World User Scenarios (2021-2022, Interaction Design and UX cluster) | User research with older adults in public parks. Its aim includes "understanding the current situation of the interaction between the context and the target population" | Observations of how people use a place over days are what a place-based diary study collects. Participants could photograph and describe their visits |
| Social Awareness and Cultural Significance in Designing (Non) Gendered Toys | A constructive design research approach that "allows the integration of qualitative processes, like ethnography" | Families could record moments of play at home, which a researcher cannot observe directly |
| A Human-Centred Ethical Framework for Addressing Privacy Issues in Service Design | Starts from the point that "Contemporary service design involves handling increasing amounts of people's information" | A platform that collects personal media is a case for exactly this framework, and its privacy decisions (file 06) can be discussed with this work |
| Doctoral work on service design and museum visitor experience (2017-2021) | Visitor experience as a service | Muskat et al. (2013) used mobile ethnography to evaluate a museum visit (file 02). This is the closest published use of the method to a UNIDCOM theme |
| Gambera, Riccò and Duarte (2022), "Pain[off]. A synaesthetic design probe" | A design probe, a method in which participants document their own experience with materials given by the researcher | Probes and diaries share the logic of participant self-documentation. A digital diary is one possible probe |

The table shows where methods overlap. It is not evidence of a request from UNIDCOM. We state it
this way in every document: UNIDCOM's research uses ethnographic, user-research and service-design methods that a diary platform could
support.

## What a unit like UNIDCOM would need from the platform

These requirements come from three places: what the unit is (a university research unit in
Portugal, funded by FCT, mostly design researchers), what the literature in files 01, 02 and 06
says about diary studies, and what the existing platforms in file 03 offer.

| Need | Reasoning | Consequence for Fieldnote |
|---|---|---|
| Data stays under the institution's control | Research with personal data in the EU needs a lawful basis, safeguards and, in practice, an ethics committee's approval (file 06). Committees ask where data is stored and who can access it. Commercial platforms are run by the vendor; Indeemo advertises "EU and US data residency options" and m-Path stores data "on Microsoft Azure servers in Frankfurt, Germany" | Fieldnote is designed to run on the team's or the institution's own machines. We infer this need from how ethics committees work, not from a request by UNIDCOM, and a vendor with EU data residency would meet part of it. Self-hosting is therefore a choice, and it is why the fault tolerance has to be built by us: the vendor no longer guarantees that no entry is lost |
| Cost that a research unit can sustain | m-Path, an academic platform, lists an Essential plan from 1599 EUR per year (excluding VAT) for 300 participants. dscout and Indeemo do not publish prices and ask for a quote | A self-hosted platform costs hardware and maintenance time. We do not claim it is cheaper overall; we claim the unit would control the cost and the data |
| Researchers who are designers, not engineers | The users of the dashboard design studies; they do not administer servers | The dashboard must hide the distributed system. Failures are visible to the team (monitoring), not to researchers |
| Rich media, not only questionnaires | Design research works with images, sound and video of objects, places and practices (file 02). Psychology-oriented tools such as m-Path focus on short questionnaires | Six entry types including photo, video and audio, with media handled by object storage and a media worker |
| Studies about places and services | SMARTOUT and the museum work are about places | Optional location per activity, off by default because location identifies people (file 06) |
| Participants in Portugal | Participants of a Lisbon-based unit are mostly Portuguese speakers | The app's interface texts and consent texts must be available in Portuguese. The archive is in English because of the course; the product cannot be English-only. This is added to the requirements |
| Several studies at once | A unit with 131 researchers can have several studies running in parallel | The scheduler plans per study; the server cap per slot is per study, and a global budget across studies is listed as future work (see `04_Documentacao_Tecnica/scheduler.md`) |

## What this framing does not allow us to claim

- That UNIDCOM commissioned Fieldnote, approved its design, or will use it. If a collaboration is
  formalised during the semester, the descriptive report will say so with its date.
- That any UNIDCOM researcher validated the requirements above. They are derived from public
  information and from the literature.
- That Fieldnote will be used in a real study during the course. A real study needs ethical
  approval and recruitment that do not fit a semester. The team may run a few small tests with
  people, such as colleagues using the app for some days, but never at the scale of a real study,
  and it promises no results from them. The demonstration uses generated data.

## How the project proceeds because of this

1. Requirements that come from the UNIDCOM framing are marked as such in the backlog, so the
   professors can see which choices come from the briefing and which from our reading of the user.
2. Self-hosting and data control are treated as a product requirement and not only as a course
   constraint. This is the argument that connects the user to the distributed design (file 08).
3. The Portuguese interface is added to the scope of the mobile app.
4. If the team gets the chance to show the platform to UNIDCOM researchers, the questions we would
   ask are already listed: which studies they would run, how long, how many participants, which
   media, which export format their analysis software needs, and what their ethics committee
   requires. The answers would replace the inferences in this file.

## Limits

- The unit's website was in transition when accessed (five clusters in one place, seven in
  another; a lab described that is not in the current list). Figures such as the number of
  researchers can change.
- We read project titles, keywords and short descriptions, not the full outputs. A UNIDCOM project
  using diary methods may exist without being described on the site.
- The rating of the 2023/2024 FCT evaluation was not found on a public page.
- The statement that UNIDCOM could use a tool of this kind comes from informal contact with the
  institution, not from a public or written document.
